# Where an order comes from

Every order has a **source**: the ad it credits, the site or app the shopper
came from, or a direct visit. Nobody chooses it by hand. The storefront
records where each visit came from, and the server decides the source when
the order is placed, with the same rules for every theme.

## What an order shows

The order page shows the source as a headline ("Instagram ad", "Google
search", "Direct visit"), then up to four parts, kept apart:

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Ad credited** | The last ad the shopper clicked, at most 7 days before ordering: campaign, ad, placement, landing page and click time. |
| **Earlier visit** | Where the shopper came from before coming back directly, when that is the source. |
| **This visit** | How the shopper came to the store the time they ordered, an ad or not. |
| **First visit** | How the shopper first found the store, when that is not already shown. |

Orders entered by your team, through the API or by an AI agent say so
instead ("Added in the dashboard"). An order with no recorded visit says
**Source unknown**, never a guess.

## How the source is decided

In this order:

1. **The ad the order credits**: the last ad click at most **7 days** before
   the order, the default click window of Meta's ads. A later visit from a
   search engine, a social network or a typed address never takes the credit
   from it.
2. Else **this visit**, when it came from somewhere: a search engine, a
   social network, an email, an AI assistant, another site or a tagged link.
3. Else **the last visit that came from somewhere**, at most **30 days**
   before the order. A shopper who finds the store on Google and comes back
   two days later by typing the address ordered "from Google search", as
   Google Analytics, Shopify and WooCommerce count it.
4. Else **this visit as it is**: usually a direct visit.

An ad clicked more than 7 days before the order is no longer an ad source: its
visit counts as what it is without the ad (an Instagram ad becomes Instagram,
a Google ad Google), and only when it is the last visit that came from
somewhere.

## Channels

The channels follow Google Analytics' default channel group:

| Channel | Means |
| --- | --- |
| `paid_social`, `paid_search`, `paid_other` | The ad the order credits, on a social network, a search engine or elsewhere |
| `organic_social` | A social network, not an ad (a post, a profile, a message link) |
| `organic_search` | A search engine, or Google's free product listings |
| `ai_assistant` | An AI assistant: ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity, Claude, DeepSeek, Grok |
| `email` | An email, or a webmail like Gmail or Outlook |
| `referral` | Another website |
| `tagged` | A link with `utm_*` parameters from no known site |
| `direct` | A typed address, a bookmark, or an app that says nothing about where it came from |
| `dashboard`, `api`, `agent` | Entered by your team, the API or an AI agent |

The orders list filters by them: `GET /api/orders?channel=paid_social,paid_search`.
`unknown` lists the orders with no recorded source.

## What makes a visit an ad

A visit is an ad click when its URL carries one of these, all added by the ad
platforms themselves, or a paid `utm_medium` (`cpc`, `ppc`, `paid…`,
`retargeting`):

| Parameter | Platform |
| --- | --- |
| `gclid`, `gbraid`, `wbraid`, `gad_source`, `gad_campaignid` | Google Ads. Google adds `gad_source` to every ad URL, so a Google ad is recognised even without its `gclid`. |
| `ttclid` | TikTok Ads |
| `msclkid` | Microsoft Advertising |
| `ScCid` | Snapchat Ads |
| `wc_cn`, `wc_an` | Wocart's URL template for Meta ads (below) |

Meta's `fbclid` alone never makes a visit an ad: Meta adds it to every link
that leaves Facebook and Instagram, ads or not. Google's `srsltid` marks
Google's free product listings, never an ad.

## Meta ads: the URL template

Meta adds no parameter that tells an ad from a post. Paste this into every
ad's **URL parameters** field in Ads Manager:

```text
utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign={{campaign.id}}&utm_term={{adset.id}}&utm_content={{ad.id}}&wc_cn={{campaign.name}}&wc_an={{ad.name}}&wc_pl={{placement}}&wc_ss={{site_source_name}}
```

Meta fills it on each click. The order then shows the campaign, the ad, the
placement and the app (Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, Threads, Audience
Network), and the ids stay readable by any analytics tool.

TikTok and Google add their own click ids (`ttclid`, `gclid`, `gad_source`)
when auto-tagging is on, which it is by default.

## What cannot be known

These are shown as **Direct visit** or **Source unknown**, never guessed:

- A shopper who taps an ad inside the Instagram or Facebook app and later
  orders from another browser: the app's browser keeps its own cookies.
- Cookies blocked, cleared, or a private window.
- Another device.
- Safari removes known click ids from links opened in Private Browsing, Mail
  and Messages. Campaign parameters (`utm_*`, and the template's `wc_*`) are
  kept, so the URL template still identifies the ad.
- Apps that open a link without saying where it came from. WhatsApp often
  does this.

## For theme developers

`@wocart/storefront` records the visits with no theme code: its middleware
sets one first-party, HttpOnly cookie (`wc_touch`, 90 days) on the response
of each page a shopper reaches from outside the store, and the order sends
it. Pages must be rendered by the middleware: an arrival page served from a
static cache records nothing. See the storefront SDK guide.
